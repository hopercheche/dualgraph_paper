EduPlanner distinguishes whole-system comparison from component attribution

EduPlanner: LLM-Based Multiagent Systems for Customized and Intelligent Instructional Design

Xueqiao Zhang, Chao Zhang, Jianwen Sun, Jun Xiao, Yi Yang, Yawei Luo · 2025
IEEE Transactions on Learning Technologies 18:416–427; reviewed version: public author manuscript arXiv:2504.05370v1, 12 pages

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (12 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to referencesHighlighted IEEE education papersDownload eight original papers and highlights

What the study investigates

EduPlanner combines evaluator, optimizer, and example-analysis agents with a competence tree for mathematics instructional design. It is an education-domain predecessor for multiagent component evaluation.

How it was studied

Section IV and Table II compare the evaluator, optimizer, their combination, additions of the analyzer or competence tree, and the complete system. Tasks use GSM8K and Algebra, and an LLM applies five CIDPP dimensions. The evaluator description mentions 100 expert-annotated instructional designs.

Findings

Table II's aggregate values are 74 for the two-agent system, 79 with the analyzer, 84 with the competence tree, and 88 for the complete system. This illustrates differences among component combinations in generated-design evaluation.

Limits of the evidence

The 100 annotated designs are not 100 students. The public manuscript does not clearly state the formal generation-test count, repeat count, or confidence intervals. Predominantly model-based assessment is not evidence of real learning gains. The table is from the complete author manuscript and has not been established as textually identical to the published layout.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

A focused ablation tests our central dependency: retain extracted events and rule content, but disable the effect of actually delivered AI hints or unauthorized disclosure on learner-assessment conditions. This avoids model fine-tuning and multiple newly built platforms. Independent faculty references evaluate the result; EGJ does not grade its own correctness.

Where to cite it

Evaluation: justify a mechanism ablation. Do not cite the paper as proof that EGJ works or as a requirement to copy every agent combination.

Possible manuscript wording

Educational multiagent studies use component combinations to examine mechanism contributions. Our targeted graph-link ablation controls model, rules, and evidence while using an independent faculty reference. [R30·1]
