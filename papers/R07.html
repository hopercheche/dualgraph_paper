R07 · Rule and evidence algorithms

GSR already compiles rubrics into deterministically executed graphs

Graph-Structured Rubrics: Compiling Rubrics into Typed Evaluation Graphs for LLM Judges

Xi Chen, Jie Mu, Mo Xuan, Qun Shao · 2026
arXiv:2608.12097; preprint

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (11 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to references

What the study investigates

Rubrics may contain hierarchies, aggregation operators, and grade caps. Putting these entirely into a prompt leaves execution order implicit and difficult to audit.

How it was studied

GSR compiles a typed directed acyclic graph before seeing the response to be judged. Models evaluate criterion leaves; Transform, Reduce, and Gate operators perform deterministic aggregation. Compilation checks ports, types, and cycles, and execution produces replayable traces. Experiments cover four item-level scoring datasets, two pairwise-preference benchmarks, and aggregation ablations.

Findings

With GPT-OSS-120B, exact agreement improves by 0.62–6.75 percentage points over a Prometheus-style scorer across the four item-level datasets. This range refers to that specified comparator; it does not establish superiority over every judge.

Limits of the evidence

Explicit gates, graph compilation, deterministic aggregation, and audit traces are established contributions of GSR. Its main objects are provided candidate responses. In pharmacy interaction, actions taken by the role generator can change the conditions under which subsequent learner evidence should be graded.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

GSR is a close algorithmic predecessor. DualGraph-OSCE should claim neither static compilation nor deterministic operators as unique inventions. Its proposed increment is the execution of dependencies between delivered role actions and assessment conditions through shared evidence. The A/B comparison and targeted linkage ablation test that increment; they do not isolate every GSR operator or establish superiority over all static graph scorers.

Where to cite it

Related work: a central algorithmic comparison. Method: compilation and aggregation. Evaluation: delimit what the planned comparison actually tests.

Possible manuscript wording

GSR expresses rubric composition as an executable graph program. DualGraph-OSCE extends this execution idea to interactive pharmacy stations, where role events and learner evidence are interpreted within the same session state. [R07·1]
