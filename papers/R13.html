R13 · Virtual patients and OSCEs

Large-scale medical dialogue rubrics do not remove the need for a faculty reference

MedDialogRubrics: A Comprehensive Benchmark and Evaluation Framework for Multi-Turn Medical Consultations in Large Language Models

Lecheng Gong, Weimin Fang, Ting Yang, Dongjie Tao et al. · 2026
arXiv:2601.03023; work-in-progress preprint

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (17 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to references

What the study investigates

Medical-dialogue evaluation must cover multiround communication and clinical requirements. The authors combine synthetic patients, controlled interaction, evidence-informed rubrics, and automated judges into a benchmark.

How it was studied

A multiagent process generates records, decomposes patient information into atomic facts, and constrains dialogue through feedback and correction. Clinical knowledge and expert annotations inform rubric generation, and an ensemble of judges evaluates responses. The public manuscript describes 5,200 cases and approximately 60,000 rubric items, with expert-agreement checks on a subset.

Findings

The paper presents a large-scale benchmark construction and model evaluation. Model-generated dialogue and rubrics improve scalability, but reliability must be checked separately at each stage.

Limits of the evidence

This is a work-in-progress preprint. A large synthetic set is not an equally large set of independent real clinical cases, and agreement among models is not clinical truth. PDF page 11 describes at most 12 turns, whereas later text discusses performance beyond 13 turns. Do not adopt a turn-count effect from that unresolved inconsistency.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

Synthetic cases can cover uncommon failure types, but their generator must not also define the reference answers used to establish correctness. Faculty should inspect the actual text and, where relevant, audio without seeing system predictions. Live consultations, synthetic minimal pairs, and manually specified events must be reported separately.

Where to cite it

Related work: medical-dialogue benchmarks. Evaluation: division of responsibilities between synthesis and independent annotation.

Possible manuscript wording

Multiround medical benchmarks already combine atomic facts, expert rubrics, and model-judge ensembles. DualGraph-OSCE evaluates fixed station-rule execution using independent faculty review and event-level perturbations. [R13·1]
