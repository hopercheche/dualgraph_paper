R18 · Evaluation and measurement

Sample size must follow the research question and acceptable uncertainty

Sample Size Justification

Daniël Lakens · 2022
Collabra: Psychology 8(1):33267; local copy: publicly available institutional author manuscript

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (29 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to references

What the study investigates

Choosing 30 participants by convention or copying another paper's sample size does not explain the effect a study can detect or the precision of its conclusions.

How it was studied

The article reviews sample-size justifications, including population sampling, resource constraints, a priori power, target estimation precision, and heuristics. It emphasizes defining a meaningful effect and explaining what a resource-limited study can still answer.

Findings

This methodological article offers no universal minimum. Confidence-interval width, the smallest effect of interest, and resource limits must be connected to the intended inference.

Limits of the evidence

It cannot justify an assertion that 16 students are sufficient in every design. Two stations completed by the same learner are correlated, and repeated runs of the same script do not create additional independent participants.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

The planned 120 frozen dialogue turns are technical inputs, not 120 independent students. A turn may share a consultation or script family with other turns; that dependence must be identified before analysis. The proposed live consultation set addresses feasibility and natural use. Technical power depends on the paired discordance assumptions and independent units actually obtained, while any learning-effect claim requires a separate participant-level justification.

Where to cite it

Evaluation: sample-size rationale, clustering, power, and the limits imposed by recruitment.

Possible manuscript wording

Sample size is justified using a prespecified effect and target precision, with within-learner and within-dialogue dependencies represented in the analysis. Resource limitations narrow the inference rather than establish a universal adequacy threshold. [R18·1]

This is a manuscript-specific paraphrase, not a quotation from the source. The excerpts below define what the citation supports. The proposed method and its expected benefits are not findings of the cited paper.
