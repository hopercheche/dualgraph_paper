IEEE education guidance asks for evidence matched to each research claim

Guest Editorial: A Practical Approach to Understanding—and Applying!—the Scholarship of Application

Barbara Oakley, Cynthia J. Finelli · 2014
IEEE Transactions on Education 57(2):69–74; editorial methodological guidance, not an empirical trial

What the study investigates

Once an educational technology has been built, how should a study demonstrate that it achieves its intended purpose? This editorial explains design and argumentation in the scholarship of application.

How it was studied

The authors use two hypothetical flipped-classroom studies to illustrate design choices. They emphasize clear claims, justified comparisons and measures, and analyses that answer the research question. The examples are not empirical student experiments; their numbers are not observed results.

Findings

The article asks researchers to explain key decisions through published work, present evidence supporting conclusions, and acknowledge limitations. Student opinion should not be the main evidence for a technical or learning claim.

Limits of the evidence

This is 2014 journal guidance, not a mandatory EDUCON 2027 review checklist. It specifies no minimum participant or ablation-group count. The broader point is transparent evidence collection, analysis, and interpretation beyond student self-report.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

DualGraph-OSCE's main claim concerns improved role control and assessment decisions, so primary evidence must compare actual outputs with a faculty reference. Learner questionnaires explain natural-use issues. Paired technical evaluation, a focused linkage ablation, and live use answer different questions; a larger questionnaire cannot replace assessment validation.

Where to cite it

Study-design rationale: separate algorithmic evidence from learner experience. Discussion: limit claims to the tested stations and intended use.

Possible manuscript wording

Following IEEE education research guidance, the study aligns claims, measures, and analyses: faculty-verified paired tasks assess system correctness, while live use provides additional feasibility evidence. [R26·1]
