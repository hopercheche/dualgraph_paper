R15 · Education and pharmacy foundations

Pharmacy argumentation already represents medication benefits and risks

Supporting Pharmacist-GP Collaboration in Medication Review Using Argumentation

Nada Boudegzdame, Karima Sedki, Jean-Baptiste Lamy · 2024
EFMI STC; Studies in Health Technology and Informatics, 58–62

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (6 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to references

What the study investigates

Pharmacists and general practitioners reviewing polypharmacy need to compare reasons for treatment changes and associated risks, beyond listing isolated interaction alerts.

How it was studied

The prototype uses IBIS to represent issues, candidate options, and arguments, and QuAD to aggregate support and attack strengths. A pharmacy ontology constrains which arguments connect to which options. Examples include renal impairment, drug interactions, and treatment alternatives. Implementation uses Python, Owlready, and Fullpy.

Findings

The paper presents an interactive graphical prototype and worked examples showing support and opposition for different options. It explicitly lacks a formal effectiveness or usability evaluation and is not a clinically outcome-validated decision tool.

Limits of the evidence

Argument graphs involving drugs, contraindications, and interactions are not an unstudied area. Weighted ranking does not enforce deterministic OSCE gates; a serious safety restriction cannot simply be averaged against other advantages.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

DualGraph-OSCE can draw on issue–option–evidence relations, with an explicit regimen identifier, patient applicability, and evidence validity period. Only evidence referring to the same option can be interpreted together. Assessment consequences still come from CUHK's approved requirements. Clinical rules verify content; they must not create additional examination criteria.

Where to cite it

Related work: acknowledge prior pharmacy argumentation. Method: the basis for clinical relation design.

Possible manuscript wording

Medication-review prototypes already combine pharmacy ontologies with supporting and opposing arguments. DualGraph-OSCE uses domain relations to bind dialogue evidence to its applicable object and connect role and assessment rules. [R15·1]
