R17 · Evaluation and measurement

Validity concerns intended interpretation and use, not certification by a high score

Validation of educational assessments: a primer for simulation and beyond

David A. Cook, Rose Hatala · 2016
Advances in Simulation 1:31

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (12 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to references

What the study investigates

Simulation studies often report reliability or satisfaction without explaining what their scores mean or which educational decisions the scores can support. This article introduces a modern assessment-validity argument.

How it was studied

The authors synthesize the Messick and Kane frameworks. Evidence includes content, response process, internal structure, relations to other variables, and consequences; inferences concern scoring, generalization, extrapolation, and decisions. The intended use is specified first, followed by evidence for its weakest assumptions.

Findings

The result is a validation approach, not a universal pass threshold. A tool may support one use but not another. Validity evidence must accumulate for the population, setting, and application.

Limits of the evidence

A high ICC or accuracy does not establish that a system can replace faculty or that learners have acquired knowledge. Performance on four fixed cases cannot establish comprehensive pharmacy competence.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

The current intended use is role control and faculty-assisted assessment in four formative stations. Faculty-approved rules provide content evidence; item-level spans and state logs provide response-process evidence; blinded faculty judgments provide external comparison. Analysis should describe differences by station, learner, and error type. High-stakes examination decisions and learning transfer require additional studies.

Where to cite it

Evaluation: the overall validity framework. Discussion: the permitted scope of the conclusions.

Possible manuscript wording

Following assessment-validity frameworks, the study gathers rule-content, response-process, and faculty-agreement evidence for its specified formative-practice use. [R17·1]
