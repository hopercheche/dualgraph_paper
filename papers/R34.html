R34 · Virtual patients and OSCEs

MedSimAI provides a complete role-simulation and evidence-grounded assessment baseline

MedSimAI: Simulation and Formative Feedback Generation to Enhance Deliberate Practice in Medical Education

Yann Hicke, Jadon Geathers, Kellen Vu et al. · LAK 2026; peer-reviewed conference paper; local full text: public author manuscript v2

Original paper / publisherPublic author full textOriginal local PDF (11 pages)Complete highlighted PDFBack to the research argument

What the study investigates

The study integrates AI standardized patients, formative assessment, and learner reflection into medical-school courses, while independently checking agreement between automated and human scoring. It is a full educational platform, not merely a chatbot.

How it was studied

Faculty at three medical schools contribute case and interface design. Structured demographic information, history, communication style, and emotional characteristics form the role system prompt. The platform supports text and realtime voice. After a session, dedicated prompts evaluate MIRS items separately with quoted evidence and JSON output; case checklists can also be used. The platform analysis includes 410 learners and 1,024 completed sessions. A separate scoring validation uses 104 real OSCE transcripts with 19 applicable MIRS items per transcript.

Findings

Independent validation reports 32.5% exact score agreement, 64.1% agreement within one score point, and 87.0% threshold-classification accuracy. These mean different things: identical scores, near scores, and classification of adequate versus inadequate performance. One institution's historical-cohort comparison is positive, whereas another participation/nonparticipation comparison finds no significant difference. The study cannot be summarized as universally improving learning outcomes.

Limits of the evidence

MIRS uses five communication-score levels, unlike our binary role compliance and exact assessment-decision criteria. The 32.5% cannot be imported as the expected accuracy of our comparator; 87.0% is not pharmacy-knowledge accuracy. A four-station trial does not replicate the three-school validation.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

A strong conventional comparator can already include faculty-structured cases, role prompts, complete transcripts, item-level rubrics, and quoted evidence. Adding citations to scores is therefore not our contribution. DualGraph-OSCE tests whether learner-evidence eligibility can share execution with next-turn role permissions and whether actually delivered hints update assessment conditions under the original faculty rules. MedSimAI does not study this specific executable dependency; that does not mean its LLM is unable to understand a hint. Use a MedSimAI-informed conventional+RAG baseline, keeping shared components and information equal rather than claiming an exact platform reproduction.

Where to cite it

Introduction: a full AI OSCE platform and the need for independent score verification. Related work: the principal platform baseline. Evaluation: separate technical validity from learner experience.

Possible manuscript wording

AI SP platforms already support faculty-structured cases and item-level assessment with quoted evidence, but independent validation distinguishes exact scoring from threshold classification. Building on these capabilities, DualGraph-OSCE tests joint execution of role-intervention conditions and learner-evidence judgments. [R34·1]
