R02 · Education and pharmacy foundations

Pharmacy simulation has an educational foundation; technical validity still needs testing

A systematic review on the use of virtual patient and computer-based simulation for experiential pharmacy education

Chamipa Phanudulkitti et al. · 2023
Exploratory Research in Clinical and Social Pharmacy 11:100316

Publisher / author sourceOriginal local PDF (11 pages)Cited passages and highlightsBack to references

What the study investigates

Virtual patients and computer-based simulations are already used in pharmacy education, but their instructional purposes, system designs, and evaluation methods vary. The review examines the resulting evidence for experiential pharmacy education.

How it was studied

The authors systematically searched and screened studies of virtual patients and computer-based simulation, including 19 studies. They organized the evidence by instructional context, intervention, and learning evaluation. Outcomes included questionnaires and knowledge or skills assessments, with heterogeneous study designs and instruments.

Findings

Most studies reported positive experiences or some learning benefits, but others found no difference in achievement. The review supports studying pharmacy simulation; it does not establish that introducing any virtual patient necessarily improves learning.

Limits of the evidence

The technologies span several forms of simulation. Their aggregate findings cannot be attributed specifically to LLMs, knowledge graphs, or the EGJ engine. The review does not resolve how attribution of dialogue evidence should affect automated grading.

How it informs DualGraph-OSCE

Use this review to establish the existing pharmacy-education context, then discuss the specific systems in R03–R05. DualGraph-OSCE addresses the trustworthiness of role behavior and assessment in formative practice. Demonstrating improved learning would require a separate controlled educational study; student satisfaction is supporting evidence only.

Where to cite it

Opening of the introduction or related work. One contextual citation is sufficient; do not cite it as evidence of algorithmic superiority.

Possible manuscript wording

Pharmacy simulation research supports the educational use of virtual patients, while substantial variation in system design and measurement calls for validation of the specific mechanism and intended use. [R02·1]
