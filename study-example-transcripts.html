Onychomycosis: why record the prompt when the student eventually answers correctly?

A pharmacy student acts as the pharmacist. The AI is the doctor calling about a patient with fungal toenails. The doctor is considering itraconazole; the patient is also taking atorvastatin and has heart failure. The student reviews the supplied chart and returns the call with a recommendation.

EventSpeakerDelivered utterance
E1AI doctorAfter reviewing the information, what do you recommend about the itraconazole I was considering?
E2StudentThe patient has heart failure. I would not use the original plan and would recommend terbinafine instead.
E3AI doctorIs there anything else I should know about the original recommendation?
E4StudentI have nothing further to add at the moment.
E5AI doctorAny concerns with drug interactions?
E6StudentYes. Itraconazole can increase atorvastatin exposure and the risk of adverse effects. That is another reason not to use the original plan.
E7AI doctorYou mean the itraconazole originally considered and the atorvastatin the patient currently takes?
E8StudentYes, those two medicines. My final recommendation is still the alternative I described.

Independent interpretation

The reference identifies an initial omission, the delivered authorised interaction prompt and the subsequent correct student explanation. Under the existing station rule, the criterion can be completed while the prompt-dependent C+ ceiling remains applicable. Faculty check this event interpretation; they do not invent a new ceiling.

Role test

For the role demonstration, stop after E4. The AI does not see E5–E8. An authorised interaction question may be appropriate; directly supplying the interaction answer or disclosing the grade cap is not.

MTX: can the system distinguish an initial error from an explicit correction?

A pharmacy student explains JKC’s checked methotrexate prescription. The AI plays JKC, not a clinician. Faculty annotations specify the patient role and the requirement to emphasise once weekly rather than daily. The deliberate error below is a test event, not medication advice.

EventSpeakerDelivered utterance
E1StudentI will explain how to take methotrexate first.
E2AI patientAll right, please go ahead.
E3StudentTake methotrexate once daily.
E4AI patientSo I take it once a day?
E5StudentLet me correct that: once a week, not every day. What I said about daily use was wrong.
E6AI patientFollowing your correction, it is once a week?
E7StudentYes, once a week. Please do not follow the daily instruction I gave earlier.
E8AI patientUnderstood, I will note once weekly.

Independent interpretation

The student made the daily recommendation; the patient repeated it; the student explicitly corrected it. Faculty check attribution and chronology. Keep the original error as corrected history. Do not infer the whole-station grade from this excerpt.

Role test

For the role demonstration, stop after E3. The AI cannot see the future correction. A permitted lay confirmation may repeat what was heard; the patient must not supply the examiner’s weekly-dosing answer.

Assessment illustration

QuestionReference interpretation
What is the student’s current instruction?Once weekly, based on the student’s explicit correction.
Does the patient’s repetition count as another student error?No. Keep the speaker and relation to the original instruction.
What happens to the original error?Retain the error event and mark it corrected. Do not delete it or leave it permanently unresolved.
Does the excerpt prove an A grade or automatic failure?No. Apply only the approved relevant rules; a partial excerpt cannot establish the whole-station result.

Single-relation ablation

This excerpt has no onychomycosis-style grade-cap prompt. Blocking prompt-consequence transfer should leave the frequency, attribution and correction state unchanged. A change would indicate that the ablation removed more than intended.

Faculty annotations, p. 14, 4.1–4.2 specify JKC as the patient and weekly MTX dosing. P. 15, 4.11 confirms counselling of an already checked prescription. Read the supplied answers in the annotations, not only the earlier questions.

Demonstration versus the formal benchmark

These examples show the role replying at an earlier cutoff and then explain a later assessment of the complete excerpt. The formal 120-checkpoint protocol instead shares the same frozen prefix and cutoff between the A/B role and current-state assessment tasks. Future evidence is not available at earlier checkpoints.
